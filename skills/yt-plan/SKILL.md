# yt-plan

A plan that doesn't fit the week is just a list of regrets. Ask two things
before writing anything: **how many hours do you really have**, and **what's
already half-made**.

## Before writing

1. Read `~/.claude/youtube/voice.md` if it's there. It's the user's voice
   profile: how they talk on camera, words they never say, who they're talking
   to, and what they won't claim. If it's missing, ask for **three of their own
   videos**, read or transcribe them, work out the voice, and write the file.
2. Never make up a number, a result or a source. If a figure would help and you
   don't have it, ask for it or write the line without it.

## What a week looks like

- **One anchor.** The video the week exists for. It gets the most time, and it
  goes out on the day the channel's own analytics say works best - ask for that
  instead of assuming Tuesday.
- **One quick one.** Built from something that already exists: a clip, a
  reaction, a follow-up to last week's most-replied comment.
- **Shorts from the anchor.** Three, cut from the long video, not written from
  scratch. `/yt-shorts` finds them.

Three uploads in a seven-day week, not seven. A daily posting plan isn't one
anyone actually sticks to, and the empty days are what let the busy ones
survive a bad week.

## What to give back

A table: day, format, working title, the one-sentence promise, and what already
exists for it. Then the honest line at the bottom: how many hours this will
take, and what to drop first if the week falls apart.

## The gate

Nothing here gets published. This skill writes and you publish. Every result
ends with a block the user copies, and the last line of every run asks:
**ship it, or change it?**
